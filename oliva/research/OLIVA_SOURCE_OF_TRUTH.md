## 19. Visual design direction

**"Warm Architectural Minimalism"** — derived from, not imposed on, the brand:

- **Terracotta plaster** (logo background) → a tactile hero/section colour and an **arch motif** (from the curved shadow in the avatar).
- **Walnut + greige + veined stone** (every project) → the material language of the UI: thin lines, stone-coloured panels, wood as the only "colour" inside photos.
- **Fashion-serif wordmark** → large editorial serif headlines, tiny widely-tracked caps labels (echoing "KITCHEN & HOME FURNITURE").
- **LED light lines** (in every kitchen) → a recurring thin glowing rule as a section divider / hover detail.
- Photography presented large and straight-on, like their own posts — architecture, not product cards.

Avoid: olive green, bright colours, rounded "app" cards, icons, stock lifestyle shots.

## 20. Competitive positioning (what can honestly differentiate the site)

Typical Egyptian kitchen sites: catalogue grids, price banners, stock photos, WhatsApp popups. OLIVA's site can differ by:
1. Real delivered work shown as architecture, one project at a time.
2. A material story told from their actual finishes (walnut, greige, veined stone, gloss).
3. A coherent brand world (terracotta plaster, arch, serif) that matches their already-premium Instagram.
4. An interactive concept configurator using only finishes seen in their kitchens, clearly labelled "Interactive concept".
No superiority claims (no "best", "No.1", years, counts).
